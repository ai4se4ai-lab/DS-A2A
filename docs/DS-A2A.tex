\title{\textsc{AgentM2M}: M2M Transformations for Agentic Collaborations in Software Engineering}

% --- Double-anonymous review: no author block in the submission. ---
% \author{...}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
LLM agent teams often fail because of the \emph{glue} between agents:
hand-offs are free text, nothing records which artifacts correspond, and
team structure is frozen at design time. We propose \tool{}, which treats every hand-off between agents
as an ATL-style model-to-model (M2M) transformation. Each agent owns a view
model conforming to its own metamodel; the \emph{structure} of a
hand-off---matching, element creation, reference resolution, trace
links---is executed by a deterministic engine, while the LLM is confined to
\emph{stochastic bindings} that fill attribute values from a bounded source
footprint and are accepted only after validation. Persisted trace models
drive change propagation, and evolving the team at runtime is a
higher-order transformation. We relate this design to
11 of the 14 MAST failure modes (5 directly, 6 partially) and report a
pilot with an open-source prototype: change impact was exact
(precision and recall 1.00) where baselines over-regenerated, and a new
agent was added at runtime with 7 lines of glue, while effects on
hand-off fidelity remain inconclusive.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
LLM-based multi-agent systems, model-to-model transformation, ATL,

\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

LLM-based agents increasingly work in \emph{teams} that jointly analyze,
design, implement, and test software~\cite{he2025lma}, organized by
roles~\cite{hong2024metagpt,qian2024chatdev,tao2024magis} or by wired
conversation topologies~\cite{wu2024autogen,li2023camel,chen2024agentverse}.
The empirical record is sobering. Cemri et al.\ analyzed more than 1{,}600
annotated traces of seven multi-agent frameworks and derived the MAST taxonomy
of 14 failure modes~\cite{cemri2025mast}. Gains over single-agent baselines
are often minimal, and 44.2\% of the failure-mode occurrences they report fall
into \emph{system design issues}, 32.3\% into \emph{inter-agent misalignment},
and 23.5\% into \emph{task verification}. Their case studies also show that
changing a team's \emph{structure} alone raises task success
substantially.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Problem analysis.} To identify what a
coordination layer could fix, we mapped each MAST failure mode to the
coordination deficit it manifests (Table~\ref{tab:mast}). The mapping is
analytical, based on the published mode definitions; it and its rationale
are part of our artifact (Section~\ref{sec:data}). Three deficits emerge:

\noindent\textbf{P1: Lossy hand-offs between heterogeneous views.} Each
agent reasons over its own representation (stories, components, code,
tests), but hand-offs are free text re-interpreted by an LLM, so information
is silently dropped or hallucinated (FM-1.4, 2.1, 2.4, 2.5).

\noindent\textbf{P2: No consistency maintenance or traceability under
change.} Nothing records which artifacts \emph{correspond}; agents act on
stale artifacts, repeat work, drift, and verify the wrong thing (FM-1.3,
2.3, 3.1--3.3).

\noindent\textbf{P3: Collaboration structure lives in prompts.} Roles,
topology, and termination are stated in prompts, so they are neither
enforced (FM-1.2, 1.5) nor changeable at runtime without re-engineering the
glue by hand.

Table~\ref{tab:mast} also marks the modes a coordination layer
\emph{cannot} address (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Existing remedies.} Interoperability protocols such
as MCP and A2A~\cite{mcp2024,a2a2025} standardize \emph{how} messages are
transported, not \emph{what} they mean. Structured
documents~\cite{hong2024metagpt} and schema-validated shared
state~\cite{zhang2026patchboard} make outputs well-formed, but a single
shared schema removes heterogeneity by fiat and leaves relations
\emph{between} perspectives implicit. Automated agent-system
design~\cite{zhuge2024gptswarm,hu2025adas,zhang2025aflow} searches topologies
offline, without guarantees about what flows along edges.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Key insight.} Managing heterogeneous views,
relating them explicitly, keeping them consistent under change, and evolving
them at runtime are core concerns of model-driven engineering (MDE): view-based
development~\cite{klare2021vitruvius,bruneliere2019views},
bidirectional transformations (bx)~\cite{czarnecki2009bx,stevens2010qvt}
and their networks~\cite{stevens2020networks,klare2023termination},
models@run.time~\cite{blair2009runtime}, and higher-order transformations
(HOTs)~\cite{tisi2009hot}. They do \emph{not} cover what defines agent teams: the
transformations between views are performed by stochastic LLMs, and the team
changes while it runs.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Our approach.} \tool{} treats every hand-off as an
ATL-style M2M transformation~\cite{jouault2008atl} and splits each rule
along a line ATL's execution model already draws: matching, element
creation, and reference resolution stay with a deterministic engine; only
the \emph{value} of a text or code attribute is delegated to an LLM, as a
validated \emph{stochastic binding} over a declared source footprint. Trace links, which ATL keeps
transiently~\cite{jouault2005traceability}, are persisted as trace
models~\cite{vara2014metagem} and become the correspondences of a
transformation network; changing the team is a HOT over a team model kept
at runtime.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} \textbf{(C1)} the formulation of
agent teams as networks of hybrid deterministic/stochastic M2M
transformations (Section~\ref{sec:approach}); \textbf{(C2)} an open-source
prototype of the \tool{} runtime over EMF-compatible metamodels with
pluggable LLM backends, released with the \devteam{} metamodels and rules
and the MAST mapping (Section~\ref{sec:data}); and \textbf{(C3)} a pilot evaluation
on DevBench against free-text and shared-schema baselines, grounded in MAST
(Section~\ref{sec:eval}).

\section{Background and Running Example}\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Model-to-model transformation with ATL}

An M2M transformation $T:\Mods{\MM_A}\to\Mods{\MM_B}$ maps models
between metamodels (classes, attributes, references, OCL
constraints)~\cite{jouault2008atl}. An ATL \code{module} consists of
\emph{matched rules}: rule
\code{from s:A!C (g) to t:B!D (f <- e)} maps every source element of type
\cls{C} satisfying OCL guard \code{g} to a target element of type \cls{D}
whose feature \code{f} is initialized by the \emph{binding} \code{f <- e}.
Execution has two phases. \emph{Matching} evaluates all source patterns,
allocates the target elements of every match, and records a \emph{trace
link} from match to targets; \emph{initialization} evaluates the bindings,
\emph{resolving} any source element a binding yields through the trace to the
target generated from it. Sources are read-only, targets write-only, and a
source element is matched by at most one rule; hence the result is
independent of rule order.

\subsection{Running example: \devteam{}}

\devteam{} is a four-agent team that builds a small to-do web service. The
\emph{Analyst} turns user requests into user stories grouped by epics, each
with acceptance criteria; the \emph{Architect} designs components and API
operations; the \emph{Developer} writes code edits; the \emph{Tester} writes
test cases with executable oracles and reports verdicts. The initial
requirements contain epic \code{E1} (\emph{task management}) with story
\code{S1} \emph{create a task} (accepted; criterion \code{S1.1}: a title is
required), story \code{S2} \emph{mark a task done} (accepted; \code{S2.1}:
completing a done task is rejected), and story \code{S3} \emph{export tasks
as CSV} (still a draft; \code{S3.1}: the file has a header row).

In a free-text team, each deficit shows up once. \emph{P1:} the Analyst's summary drops \code{S2.1}, so no operation handles
double completion and no test checks it (FM-2.4, surfacing as FM-3.2).
\emph{P2:} the Analyst later tightens \code{S2.1} to ``return HTTP 409'';
nothing says which signature, code edit, or test depends on it, so the team
either regenerates everything or keeps stale artifacts (FM-1.3). \emph{P3:}
midway, the lead wants a security review of every API operation, which
requires rewiring prompts and routing by hand.

\tool{} is used in seven steps (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). Steps~1--3 are
design time; Steps~4--7 are executed by the runtime in
Algorithm~\ref{alg:runtime}, whose circled numbers refer to the steps.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Step~1: Give every agent its own form (view
metamodel).} Every artifact an agent $a_i$ produces is a model
$M_i\in\Mods{\MM_i}$ conforming to a view metamodel $\MM_i$ owned by $a_i$.
This ``form'' fixes what the agent can state
(Listing~\ref{lst:views}, KM3-like syntax).

\begin{lstlisting}[language=KM3, float=t,
  caption={View metamodels of the \devteam{} agents.},
  label={lst:views}]
package Req {                            -- Analyst
 enumeration Status { literal draft; literal accepted; }
 class Epic      { attribute name : String; }
 class UserStory { attribute id, title : String;
   attribute status : Status;  reference epic : Epic;
   reference criteria[1-*] container : Criterion; }
 class Criterion { attribute id, text : String; } }
package Arch {                           -- Architect
 class Component { attribute name : String; }
 class Operation { attribute name, signature : String;
   reference component : Component; } }
-- Code: CodeEdit(file, body)
-- Test: TestCase(name, oracle), TestRun(name, verdict)
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent In Listing~\ref{lst:views}, \cls{Operation} knows nothing about
user stories and \cls{TestCase} nothing about operations: views stay
heterogeneous; how they \emph{correspond} is produced by hand-offs and
recorded in trace models (Step~6), not baked into a shared schema.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Step~2: Describe the team as a model.} A team is a
megamodel $\team=(A,V,\mathbf{T},\omega,\varphi)$ of agents $A$, views
$V=\{\MM_i\}$, hand-off transformations $\mathbf{T}$, write rights
$\omega:A\to 2^{V}$, and an acceptance predicate $\varphi$ (Step~7). For
\devteam{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}), $A=\{\mathit{an},\mathit{ar},
\mathit{dv},\mathit{ts}\}$, $\omega(\mathit{ar})=\{\MM_{\mathit{arch}}\}$
and similarly for the others, and $\mathbf{T}$ contains
$T_1$ (\code{Req2Arch}), $T_2$ (\code{Arch2Code}), $T_3$ (\code{Code2Test},
one \cls{TestRun} per code edit), and $T_4$ (\code{Criterion2Test}).
Hand-offs into the same view create disjoint element types (\cls{TestRun}
vs.\ \cls{TestCase}), so no target element has two producers. $T_2$ is
multi-source: the Developer reads $M_{\mathit{arch}}$ and
$M_{\mathit{req}}$. $\omega$ transfers ATL's discipline to agents: an agent
reads upstream views and writes only its own. As a model, $\team$ can be
transformed while the team runs (Section~\ref{subsec:hot}).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Step~3: Write each hand-off as hybrid rules.}
A hand-off $T_{ij}:\Mods{\MM_i}\to\Mods{\MM_j}$ is a set of ATL-style rules
$r=(p_r,g_r,t_r,B_r)$: a source pattern $p_r$ with OCL guard $g_r$, a target
pattern $t_r$, and bindings $B_r$. We write $T_{ij}$ for readability; a
hand-off may read several views (as $T_2$ does), with matches ranging over
their disjoint union.

\begin{definition}[Hybrid hand-off]\label{def:hybrid}
A hand-off $T=(R^{\str},B^{\sem},\mathcal{L},\mathit{chk})$ consists of the
structural part $R^{\str}$ (patterns, guards, target patterns, and OCL
bindings of all rules), stochastic bindings $B^{\sem}$, the LLM
$\mathcal{L}$ of the target view's owner, inducing a distribution
$\Dist{\pi}$ over values for each prompt $\pi$, and a validator
$\mathit{chk}_b$ per $b\in B^{\sem}$.
\end{definition}

\noindent Thus $B_r=B_r^{\str}\uplus B_r^{\sem}$, with structural bindings
in plain OCL. A stochastic binding $b\in B_r^{\sem}$ has the form
\code{f <- @llm(prompt, e)}; the OCL expression $e$ defines the
\emph{footprint} $\fp_b(m)$ of a match $m$: the set of source locations
(object, feature) that evaluating $e$ reads, whose values are the only
source data the LLM may see. \code{@check} names the validator
$\mathit{chk}_b$ (a type/OCL constraint, a parser, or an executable oracle).
Stochastic bindings target primitive-typed attributes (strings, code
bodies), never references. Listing~\ref{lst:rules} shows $T_1$ and $T_4$
for \devteam{}.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=ATL, float=t,
  caption={Hybrid hand-offs $T_1$ and $T_4$ (\code{@llm}, \code{@check}).},
  label={lst:rules}]
module Req2Arch; create OUT : Arch from IN : Req; -- T1
rule Epic2Component { from e : Req!Epic
 to c : Arch!Component ( name <- e.name ) }
rule Story2Operation {
 from s : Req!UserStory
          (s.status = #accepted)        -- guard
 to  op : Arch!Operation (
   name      <- s.title.toOpName(),     -- structural
   component <- s.epic,          -- resolved via trace
   signature <- @llm('Derive an API signature',
          Sequence{s.title, s.criteria}), -- footprint
   @check signature.parses()
          and signature.params->notEmpty() ) }
module Criterion2Test; create OUT:Test from IN:Req; -- T4
rule Criterion2TestCase {
 from c : Req!Criterion
   (c.refImmediateComposite().status = #accepted)
 to  tc : Test!TestCase ( name <- c.id,
   oracle <- @llm('Write a test oracle', c.text),
   @check oracle.compiles() and oracle.failsOn(stub) ) }
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent In \code{Story2Operation} (Listing~\ref{lst:rules}), whether
$\mathit{op}_{S2}$ exists, its name, and its component are deterministic; the
LLM writes only its signature, from \code{S2}'s title and criteria alone.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\tool{} runtime (\step{n}: Step~n).}
\label{alg:runtime}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require team $\team$, models $M_i$, traces $\TL_{ij}$, budget $k$
\State $E\gets\emptyset$;\ $D\gets\mathbf{T}$
  \Comment{escalations; hand-offs to run}
\Loop
  \While{$D\neq\emptyset$} \Comment{\step{4}\,\step{6}}
    \State take $T_{ij}$ from $D$; run $R^{\str}$; update $\TL_{ij}$
      \label{alg:struct}
    \ForAll{stochastic binding $(t,b)$ with a stale stamp}
      \label{alg:obl}
      \State \textsc{Bind}: sample $\le k$ values from $\fp_b$ until
        $\mathit{chk}_b$, else $E\gets E\cup\{(t,b)\}$ \label{alg:bind}
    \EndFor
    \State \textbf{if} $M_j$ changed \textbf{then} $D\gets D\cup\mathit{readers}(M_j)$
  \EndWhile
  \State \textbf{if} $\varphi$ \textbf{then return} done;
    \textbf{if} $E\neq\emptyset$ \textbf{then return} $E$
    \Comment{\step{7}}
  \State $\Delta\gets\textsc{Lift}(x,M_i)$ for text $x$ of agent $a$,
    $\MM_i\in\omega(a)$ \label{alg:lift} \Comment{\step{5}}
  \State \textbf{if} $\Delta=\bot$ \textbf{then} $E\gets E\cup\{x\}$
    \textbf{else} apply $\Delta$; $D\gets\mathit{readers}(M_i)$
\EndLoop
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Step~4: Run a hand-off.}
Line~\ref{alg:struct} of Algorithm~\ref{alg:runtime} is ATL's two-phase
execution, made incremental~\cite{jouault2010incremental}: it matches on the read-only source, creates
targets and trace links for new matches, deletes those of vanished matches,
and evaluates structural bindings. Lines~\ref{alg:obl}--\ref{alg:bind}
re-bind every stochastic binding whose \emph{version stamp}---a digest of its
footprint at acceptance---is missing or stale, prompting with the footprint
only and escalating after $k$ rejections. Bindings are then checked
jointly against $\MM_j$'s OCL constraints (e.g., signatures agree on
\code{TaskId}); a violation escalates the bindings involved. On $T_1$, \code{S3} fails the
guard; $\mathit{comp}_{E1}$, $\mathit{op}_{S1}$, $\mathit{op}_{S2}$ and
three trace links are created. For $\mathit{op}_{S2}$, a sample \code{markTaskDone()}
fails the \code{params} check; \code{markTaskDone(id: TaskId): Task} is
accepted and stamped. On $T_4$, \code{S3.1} fails the guard, while
\code{S2.1} cannot be dropped: \code{Criterion2TestCase} matches it, so its
test case exists by construction (P1).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Step~5: Turn free text into models.}
Agents still think in text. \textsc{Lift} (line~\ref{alg:lift}) is the only
way text enters the system: an LLM renders it as elements of the author's own
view, rejected unless $M_i\oplus\Delta$ conforms to $\MM_i$ with its OCL
constraints. A requested story that lacks its mandatory \code{epic} fails
conformance and is resampled. Agents add structure beyond the hand-off
skeleton this way (the Architect adds a \cls{Store} component); hand-off
targets are engine-owned and read-only to agents, and lifted elements
survive re-runs. \textsc{Lift} creates \emph{source} structure
for downstream hand-offs, never structure \emph{inside} one, so the
target elements, references, and trace links of each hand-off remain a
function of its source models alone.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Step~6: Handle change with trace models and
obligations.} \tool{} persists each trace as a trace
model~\cite{vara2014metagem}
$\TL_{ij}\subseteq\mathit{Match}(M_i)\times M_j\times R$ with footprints and
stamps. A change $\Delta$ on $M_i$, modifying locations
$\mathit{foot}(\Delta)$, re-runs $R^{\str}$ deterministically and
yields the \emph{obligations}
\begin{align*}
\Obl(\Delta)=\{(t,b)\mid{}&(m,t,r)\in \TL_{ij},\ b\in B_r^{\sem},\\
  &\fp_b(m)\cap \mathit{foot}(\Delta)\neq\emptyset\},
\end{align*}
i.e., the stale stamps of line~\ref{alg:obl}, each addressed to the owner
of $\MM_j$. When the Analyst tightens \code{S2.1},
$\Obl=\{(\mathit{op}_{S2},\code{signature}),
(\mathit{tc}_{S2.1},\code{oracle}), (\mathit{ed}_{S2},\code{body})\}$:
$T_2$'s \code{body} binding reads $\mathit{op}.\code{signature}$ and
\code{S2}'s criteria, so the code edit is obligated directly, even if the
re-sampled signature is unchanged (a 409 need not alter it).
$\mathit{op}_{S1}$ and $\mathit{tc}_{S1.1}$, with disjoint footprints, are
untouched. Each re-accepted value is itself a change on $M_j$, so
obligations propagate hop by hop, and an identical re-sample stops only the
paths it alone determines. As in bx
networks~\cite{stevens2020networks}, for cyclic networks we schedule $D$ with
the conservative strategy of Klare and Gleitze, which provably terminates but
may fail~\cite{klare2023termination}; failures are escalated (FM-1.3, P2).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Step~7: Know when the team is done.} The engine,
not an agent, decides $\varphi$: every match is covered by a trace link, no
obligation or escalation is open, all stamps are fresh, and all validators
pass, including the oracles the engine executes for each \cls{TestRun}
created by $T_3$, whose verdicts it records. A Tester declaring success
early cannot end the run while $\mathit{tc}_{S2.1}$'s new oracle fails
(FM-1.5, 3.1): termination is a property of the models, not a claim in a
conversation.

\subsection{Evolving the team with higher-order transformations}
\label{subsec:hot}

MeTAGeM-Trace turns relation models between metamodels, via HOTs, into
trace-generating ATL~\cite{vara2014metagem}; \tool{} applies this chain at
runtime, with the relation model lifted (Step~5) from the lead's request. The team model $\team$ is kept causally connected to the running
team (models@run.time~\cite{blair2009runtime}). The request of
Section~\ref{sec:background}, \emph{add a Security Reviewer with view
$\MM_{\mathit{sec}}$ related to \cls{Operation}}, is a relation model that a
HOT~\cite{tisi2009hot} turns into a trace-generating hand-off $T_5$, a new
entry in $\omega$, and a strengthened $\varphi$ (dashed in
Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). The HOT is applied between iterations of the outer
loop of Algorithm~\ref{alg:runtime}, adding $T_5$ to $D$. Because $T_5$'s
rules match existing models, the reviewer receives obligations for
$\mathit{op}_{S1}$ and $\mathit{op}_{S2}$ on arrival---no hand-written glue
(P3).

\section{Preliminary Evaluation and Discussion}\label{sec:eval}

\noindent\textbf{Setup.} Our prototype (C2) instantiates \devteam{}'s four
views and four hand-offs. We compare it with \emph{free-text} hand-offs and
a PatchBoard-style \emph{shared schema}~\cite{zhang2026patchboard} under
identical roles, prompts, and LLM, on three open-weight models (\texttt{qwen2.5-coder:7b},
\texttt{devstral:24b}, \texttt{qwen3-coder:30b}). We pilot on one Python
repository (\texttt{chakin}) of DevBench~\cite{li2024devbench}.
\textbf{RQ1 (fidelity):} does \tool{} reduce P1 failure modes?
\textbf{RQ2 (change):} how precise and complete is change impact under
three injected PRD changes (tighten, add, remove a criterion)?
\textbf{RQ3 (evolution):} can a HOT alone add an agent mid-run?


\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Results.} \emph{RQ1 is inconclusive.} No
configuration retained a canary or passed the acceptance test
(a scripted backend passes 5/5, so the harness works). P1 modes per trace averaged 1.33 (free text), 0.33 (shared schema),
and 1.00 (\tool{}), and the ranking flips by model (Fig.~\ref{fig:rq1rq2}a).
\emph{RQ2 is the strongest result.} \tool{} reached recall and precision
1.00 on all 9 trials (3 models $\times$ 3 changes), as the obligation
operator predicts by construction. Shared schema had recall 1.00 but
precision 0.23 (it regenerates everything); free-text precision was 1.00,
0.75, and 0.23, non-monotonic in model size (Fig.~\ref{fig:rq1rq2}b).
\emph{RQ3:} identically across models, the added reviewer covered all 9
existing operations with 7 glue lines versus an estimated 22 manually. \emph{The price is tokens:} 25.5k per RQ1 task
versus 14.9k (free text) and 4.8k (shared schema) (Fig.~\ref{fig:rq1rq2}c),
and 34.0k per RQ2 change versus 0.23k and 0k.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Discussion and threats.} Results on one
repository are a feasibility signal, not a powered comparison: a null RQ1
cannot separate ``no effect'' from ``underpowered.'' RQ2 and RQ3 follow
from structure, not generation quality, hence their stability across
models; \tool{}'s case rests on exact impact and runtime evolution, not
cheaper generation. Our MAST annotator
approximates an unreleased tool with an LLM of the same class it evaluates
and may be miscalibrated for \tool{}'s per-operation transcripts. Modes rooted
in one agent's reasoning (FM-1.1, 2.2, 2.6; Table~\ref{tab:mast}) are
outside a coordination layer, and \textsc{Lift} remains a free-text
boundary: content it omits is invisible to every check.

\section{Future Plans}\label{sec:future}

Towards a full paper, we will address three research challenges (RC).
\RC{1}~\emph{Learning the glue:} view metamodels and relation models are
costly to author; an architect agent will propose them, admitted only if
they conform to a meta-metamodel and pass the model checks that
MeTAGeM-Trace bundles~\cite{vara2014metagem}.
\RC{2}~\emph{Bidirectionality:} downstream agents also edit upstream intent
(a developer discovers an infeasible criterion); we will lift hand-offs to
bx and characterize correctness and hippocraticness~\cite{stevens2010qvt}
when the backward direction is stochastic. \RC{3}~\emph{Evidence at
scale:} we will extend the pilot to all 10 Python repositories of
DevBench~\cite{li2024devbench} with paired statistical tests, and run
MetaGPT- and ChatDev-style teams~\cite{hong2024metagpt,qian2024chatdev}
re-implemented on \tool{}, treating a failure to reduce P1 modes versus a
shared-schema baseline as refuting our central claim; two independent
coders will re-code the MAST mapping (Cohen's $\kappa$). We will also
compare footprint-bounded with full-context prompting, since footprints
trade context for sound change impact.
